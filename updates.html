An idle room now costs nothing 30 Aug 2026

  • Silence is no longer sent. Muting zeroed the samples and then shipped the zeroes, so someone sitting there doing nothing cost every other person the same 9.2 Mbps as someone talking. Runs of exact digital silence are now dropped after a short tail. The receiver already fills an unsent stretch with zeroes, so what it plays is still bit-identical to what was captured — an idle sender measures 0 Mbps, a speaking one still measures 9.27.
  • Quality now fits the room. A mesh sends everything once per other person, so eight people at 24-bit / 192 kHz was 64.5 Mbps of upstream each. The setting is a ceiling and the best tier that fits an upstream budget is used instead: eight people land at 16.1 Mbps, two people are untouched. Peers report loss back to whoever is sending to them, so an optimistic budget gets corrected.
  • The send queue is measured in milliseconds. It was 256 KB, which is 1.4 seconds of 16-bit / 48 kHz but only 227 ms of 24-bit / 192 kHz — and that queue was most of the half-second of delay a full room saw. Now 60 ms of audio at whatever rate is in use.
  • The camera stopped looking soft. It was hinted as motion, which tells the encoder it may trade away spatial detail, and set to balanced, which lets it drop resolution. Both are now the other way round. "No ceiling" also sets a number nothing reaches rather than leaving the maximum unset — absent is not unlimited, it falls back to a conservative default. Content hint, codec, and the resolution-versus-frame-rate trade are all selectable.
  • A kill switch in config.js, which is neither bundled nor fingerprinted, so service can be stopped from GitHub's web UI in about thirty seconds without a deploy.

Found by a friend of the author, who joined a room eight times over and did nothing at all — which turned out to be the most expensive thing anyone could have done.

Uncompressed audio, uncapped screen 30 Aug 2026

  • Lossless audio, up to 24-bit / 192 kHz. Raw PCM captured on an audio worklet and sent down its own data channel, unordered and unretransmitted, with a jitter buffer keyed by absolute frame index at the far end. No encoder anywhere in the path. Five settings, from 16-bit / 48 kHz at 1.5 Mbps to 32-bit float / 192 kHz at 12.3 Mbps. Measured on the wire, each lands within 0.7% of its theoretical raw bitrate.
  • Screen sharing to 16K at 360 fps, with ceilings up to 400 Mbps or none, selectable codec, and maintain-resolution so text stays readable under load instead of the browser trading pixels away for frame rate.
  • Camera at native resolution, up to 240 fps, no bitrate ceiling by default.
  • Remaster. An optional mastering chain on your own signal — high-pass, corrective EQ, subtractive de-esser, glue compressor, limiter — with Voice, Music and Broadcast presets. Off by default, because it means what you send is no longer raw.
  • Compressed and Voice modes for when the bandwidth is not there: Opus at 510 kbps stereo with DTX off, or 64 kbps mono.
  • Rooms are a code, not an account. Nine characters from an alphabet with nothing you could misread. Share the link and it drops people straight in.
  • Full mesh, and it survives losing the host. Nothing is relayed or mixed — which is what keeps the audio uncompressed end to end — and if whoever opened the room leaves, the rest elect someone to take the door key.

Built on the same footing as dropline: static files on GitHub Pages, PeerJS's public broker for the handshake only, and no server that ever sees the call.

Next

Roughly in order. Nothing here is promised.

  1. A TURN relay

    Around 10–20% of network pairs cannot reach each other on STUN alone. Until there is one deployed, those calls fail rather than degrade, which is the worst way to fail.

  2. Adaptive lossless

    The quality setting is fixed for the call. It should notice when the link cannot carry 9.2 Mbps and step down a tier rather than letting the jitter buffer starve.

  3. Per-person volume

    The receiver already has a gain node per peer. It just has nothing driving it yet.

  4. Recording

    The samples arriving are already the samples that were captured, so writing them to a WAV costs nothing but the plumbing.

  5. Its own signalling

    The public PeerJS broker only ever sees the handshake, never the call, but it is a shared free service with no uptime guarantee.
